# Teams triage

## Collection and catch-up

Never limit collection to messages received on the current date.

1. Enumerate all accessible one-to-one chats, relevant group chats, and mentions rather than only chats active today.
2. Use unread and last-read markers to fetch all unreviewed messages regardless of age when the connector exposes them.
3. Also inspect at least the previous seven calendar days to catch actionable messages that were marked read or missed during one or more absent workdays.
4. Continue further back when an unread marker, unresolved thread, explicit commitment, or retained follow-up indicates older work.
5. Paginate until the required scope is exhausted. If the connector caps history or lacks unread state, state the earliest timestamp actually covered and do not describe Teams as fully checked.

Within that scope, review:

- one-to-one chats;
- direct questions in active group chats;
- mentions of Michael;
- conversations containing an explicit commitment, deadline, decision, or request.

Do not dump historical chat content. Surface only unhandled, unresolved, or materially changed work.

## Suppression

Ignore unless they contain concrete work:

- stale meeting chats;
- bots and workflow notifications;
- meeting recordings and transcripts;
- reactions, greetings, acknowledgements, and casual exchanges;
- Michael's own messages returned by broad mention search.

## Decision test

Surface a Teams item only when a reasonable reader can answer: **What is Michael expected to decide, answer, or follow up on?** If that cannot be established, omit it or classify it as a useful update.

When sending, use the existing chat where possible, verify participants, and match the recipient's established language. Do not change the language used with Michael unless he asks.
